## 英文引导词

### 2. Meet the cube

Look at these three faces. Each one points in a different direction. A cube has twelve edges and eight corners. Tap each visible face and notice how they fit together. These are the landmarks we will use when we draw.

### 3. Follow the light

Now move the sun. See how each flat face catches a different amount of light? The shadow falls away from the light. Unlike a sphere, a cube changes tone at an edge. Try moving the light from one side to the other.

### 4. Your pencil toolkit

Let’s get your pencils ready. Two H for light construction. H B for sketching and gentle finishing. Two B, three B, four B, and six B give us a range of darker tones. Find them in your kit. There is no hurry. Press continue when you have them.

### 5. Blend, soften, lift

This is a blending stump. Its pointed paper tip helps you soften graphite. A folded tissue works too. Put your sketch paper and eraser beside you. We will blend gently, so the light and dark faces stay separate.

### 6. Feel your sketch paper

Let’s look at your sketch paper. Run your fingers gently over both sides. One side usually feels rougher, and the other smoother. You can draw on either side. Today, choose the rougher side. Those tiny bumps, called tooth, catch the graphite and give your pencil marks a lovely texture. Tap the paper to turn it over, then place the rougher side facing up.

### 7. Make room for your drawing

We are using A four sketch paper, twenty-one by twenty-nine point seven centimetres. But you can use another size if you prefer. Just adjust the size of your cube and leave some breathing room around it. Set your paper down with the rougher side facing up. Ready? Let’s get comfortable and draw.

### 8. Settle in

Get comfortable, with enough light on your paper. Keep your pencil pressure light at first. Remember the light face, midtone, dark face, reflected light, contact shadow, and cast shadow. We will find them as we go. I will pause at each key moment and wait while you draw. Ready? Let’s begin.

### 9. Find your space

Make four light marks to place your cube. Leave some breathing room around it. You do not need a perfect outline yet. I’ll wait here while you find its size and position.

### 10. Measure the height

Hold your pencil at arm’s length and close one eye. Compare the height with the width. Mark those proportions lightly on your paper. Take your time, then continue when you are ready.

### 11. Build the top face

Look at the top face. Use light guide lines to compare the two directions, then adjust them to match the perspective you see. Edges going in the same direction recede together. Try your top face now. I’ll wait.

### 12. See through the cube

Imagine that the cube is transparent. Lightly draw the hidden edges, and check how the back corners connect to the front. This is your framework. Finish this small part, then press continue.

### 13. Check your directions

Before you darken anything, compare the edges that travel in the same direction. Let them recede consistently into space. Adjust one line at a time. Continue when your framework feels steady.

### 14. Your framework is ready

Check the corners and the directions of your edges. Keep any corrections light. Finish your framework at your own pace, then we will give it some light and shadow.

### 15. Start with the dark face

Pick up your four B pencil. Start on the darkest face, with strokes travelling in one direction. Try a small area first. I’ll wait while you get that first layer down.

### 16. Cross the first layer

Now change the direction of your strokes and lay a second layer over the first. Use gentle pressure. This will fill the gaps without scratching the paper. Try it, then continue.

### 17. Fill the dark face

Bring that dark tone across the whole face. Aim for an even layer, and stay within the edges. I’ll wait here while you finish this plane.

### 18. Meet the light-dark edge

Notice where the two faces meet. Strengthen the dark side of that boundary a little. Keep it part of the shaded face, rather than drawing a heavy outline all around the cube. Give it a try.

### 19. Give the cube a shadow

Now add the shadow on the table. Watch its shape and the direction it travels away from the light. Keep your strokes controlled. I’ll wait while you lay it in.

### 20. Keep the light face light

Switch to two B and use a lighter touch on the gray face. Compare it with the dark face as you work. They should stay clearly different. Continue when you have a gentle, even layer.

### 21. Unify your marks

Use H B with very light pressure to tidy the surface. Sweep over the rough patches without making the whole face darker. Work slowly. I’ll wait for you.

### 22. Blend without losing the form

Use your tissue or blending stump very gently. Soften the pencil marks within each face. Keep the meeting edge clear, and leave the lightest face bright. Take your time, then continue.

### 23. Your cube has volume

You have built the core cube: three faces and a cast shadow. Take a moment to enjoy that. The next parts are optional refinements. Continue to explore them, or use the map to go to your drawing check.

### 24. Let the light face emerge

Watch how darkening the background helps the top face look brighter. Let one outer edge soften into that background. Try a small area first, and compare before you add more.

### 25. Strengthen the shadow

Add another light layer in the shadow area. Compare it with the light face. We are increasing the contrast, not pressing hard on the pencil. I’ll wait while you make that adjustment.

### 26. Refine the meeting edge

Return to the edge where the cube’s light and dark faces meet. Deepen the dark face beside that edge just enough to strengthen the form. Keep looking at both faces together. Try it now.

### 27. Check the whole drawing

Step back a little. Compare the light top, the gray face, and the dark face. Keep those three values distinct while making the marks inside each face feel even. Adjust any patch that jumps out.

### 28. Soften the pencil texture

Take your tissue or blending stump and follow the direction of your marks. Sweep lightly. We are smoothing the texture, not rubbing away the difference between the faces. I’ll wait while you finish.

### 29. Review the contrast

Look at the cube as a whole. Keep the top light, and check the contrast between the other two faces. I’ll wait while you make any final adjustments.

### 30. Ground your cube

Notice how the background gives the cube a place to sit. Keep the tone around the light face soft and controlled. Finish this stage, then continue to check your drawing.

### 31. Look at what you made

Check your perspective, your three values, and the cast shadow. Take a photo in even light. In this design preview, the photo stays on this page. Your regular lesson is still the place to submit it to your teacher.

### 32. Your final polish

For a final polish, compare the background, the meeting edge, and the softer outer edges. Use a tissue or blender gently where the marks need unifying. Stop when the drawing feels clear. I’ll wait for you.

### 33. Take this with you

You have practised perspective, separate planes, and a shadow that grounds the form. Look for those same ideas in a box, a room, or a building. If you have drawn a sphere, compare its curved shading with these flat faces. What helps your drawing feel alive? Keep that question with you.

### 34. Cube explorer

You stayed with the process, one small step at a time. That is something to feel good about. Your preview journey is complete. Keep drawing, and keep noticing.
