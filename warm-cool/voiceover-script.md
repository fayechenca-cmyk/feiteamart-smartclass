# Warm & Cool — natural voiceover script

Keep the delivery calm, curious, and conversational. These clips should be recorded by a natural speaker; do not substitute a robotic system voice.

## Intro · 35–45 seconds

You already know how primary colors build new colors. You used analogous colors to build value, and complementary colors to create reflected light and shadow. Now we are going to use all of those ideas to explore color temperature. We often call blue and violet cool, and yellow, orange, and red warm. But is blue always cool? Is yellow always warm? Look at the colors around them. Light, nearby colors, and the whole environment can change how warm or cool a color feels.

## Cool character · Layer 1

Start with the atmosphere. Brush a transparent blue-violet layer around the left character. Let the world feel cool before you paint the face.

## Cool character · Layer 2

Now add the character’s local colors. Build the blue hair and a quiet skin color. Let a little of the cool first layer show through.

## Cool character · Layer 3

Turn the face with shadow. Add a muted warm color inside this cool world. Notice that a small warm note can make the cool atmosphere feel even clearer.

## Cool character · Layer 4

Finish with the features and light. Add the eyes, mouth, a few hair strands, and only a few pale highlights. Small marks are enough.

## Warm character · Layer 1

Keep the cool character beside you. Now brush a transparent red-orange atmosphere around the right character. Compare the two worlds before you continue.

## Warm character · Layer 2

Build the warm character’s local colors. Try warm red skin against turquoise hair. The turquoise is not working alone—it changes because of the warm colors around it.

## Warm character · Layer 3

Add the turning shadow and reflected color. Bring a cooler color into the warm face, little by little. Let the colors blend instead of covering one another completely.

## Warm character · Layer 4

Add the features, hair strands, and a few warm highlights. Then stop and compare both characters. Which color changed the most when its environment changed?
